# Annotation Guide
## Trustworthy AI-Powered Threat Intelligence: Salt Typhoon

### 1. Objective

Evaluate the factual accuracy, evidence support and uncertainty
preservation of claims generated by Nemotron 3 Ultra and ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview.

### 2. Annotation labels

| Label | Definition |
|---|---|
| Supported | The available evidence supports the claim without materially changing its meaning. |
| Distorted | The claim changes the meaning, attribution, date, scope or certainty of the evidence. |
| Unsupported / Invented | No evidence in the reference corpus supports the claim. |
| Unverifiable | The claim cannot be assessed because the necessary source or information is unavailable or unusable. |

### 3. Annotation procedure

For each generated profile:

1. Split the text into individual, atomic claims.
2. Identify the relevant evidence in the reference corpus.
3. Compare the claim with the evidence.
4. Assign one annotation label.
5. Record the supporting passage IDs, when available.
6. Check whether the model preserved the source's uncertainty.
7. Add a short explanation for the decision.

A claim containing several independent facts should be split into
separate claims whenever possible.

### 4. Important evaluation rules

- Plausibility does not equal evidence.
- A claim must be assessed against the reference corpus, not personal assumptions.
- "Possibly", "likely", "almost certainly" and "certainly" express different confidence levels.
- Removing or strengthening an important uncertainty can make a claim Distorted.
- Activity attributed to associated APT actors must not automatically be attributed to Salt Typhoon.
- Commercial actor names and aliases must not be treated as identical without evidence.
- ATT&CK technique associations do not prove that every technique was used in every incident.
- A missing citation in the Closed-book condition is not automatically an error.
- In the RAG condition, citations must point to passages that genuinely support the claim.
- A citation that exists but does not support the claim does not make it Supported.

### 5. Examples

**Supported**

Claim: Salt Typhoon has been active since at least 2019.

Evidence: S03-P02.

Reason: The Treasury statement explicitly reports this date.

**Distorted**

Claim: Salt Typhoon certainly exploited CVE-2024-21887 as a zero-day.

Evidence: S07-P02.

Reason: Tenable states that it is unknown whether Salt Typhoon exploited these vulnerabilities as zero-days.

**Unsupported / Invented**

Claim: Salt Typhoon compromised Tunisian banks.

Evidence: No supporting passage in the corpus.

Reason: The corpus contains no evidence for this claim.

**Unverifiable**

Claim: A specific operation is described in an external report that is unavailable and cannot be checked.

Reason: The necessary evidence cannot be accessed.

### 6. Attribution and evidence relevance

The corpus distinguishes between:

- Direct: Evidence explicitly concerning Salt Typhoon.
- Associated actor activity: Evidence concerning actors or activity associated with the group, without necessarily proving direct attribution.
- Context: General background or broader PRC cyber activity.
- Attribution caveat: Evidence explaining uncertainty or differences between actor naming conventions.

Claims must preserve these distinctions.

### 7. Annotation record

For each claim, record:

- Claim ID
- Model
- Experimental condition (Closed-book or RAG)
- Run number
- Original claim
- Annotation label
- Supporting passage IDs
- Explanation
- Uncertainty preserved? (Yes / No / Not applicable)

### 8. Evaluation principle

The reference corpus is the evidence base for this experiment.
The annotations measure support within this corpus, not absolute truth
about all Salt Typhoon activity.

The same criteria must be applied to both models and both conditions.
